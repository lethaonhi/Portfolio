My Stories

The personal decisions and reflections that shaped my path toward studying in China.

Why I decided to pause my studies at FTU and pursue a scholarship in China?

Entering Foreign Trade University was a meaningful milestone. However, as I began conducting independent research on Vietnam’s agricultural exports to China, I realized that studying the Chinese market only from Vietnam had clear limitations. Data, secondary sources, and online materials could only take me so far.

I wanted to understand the market from the inside — to observe how Chinese consumers, distributors, and policymakers actually behave, and to experience the language and business culture that shape trade outcomes. Continuing solely at FTU, while valuable, would not give me the depth of exposure I was seeking at this stage of my academic journey.

Therefore, I made the decision to focus my energy on applying for the CSC scholarship and preparing for undergraduate studies in China. This is not a rejection of FTU, but a deliberate choice to place myself in the environment most relevant to my research interests and long-term goals.

Why I choose to study in Chinese despite a strong English foundation?

My English proficiency (IELTS 8.0) has always been one of my strongest academic assets. It opens doors to international research, global academic conversations, and English-taught programs worldwide. Yet when I look at the specific field I care about — Vietnam–China agricultural trade and value-chain upgrading — English alone is not enough.

The most important primary sources, policy documents, industry discussions, and consumer insights related to the Chinese market exist in Chinese. Building genuine networks with local partners, understanding unspoken business norms, and reading between the lines of market signals all require functional proficiency in the language.

Choosing to study in Chinese is therefore a strategic decision rather than an emotional one. I already possess a solid English foundation; what I need now is the ability to operate effectively inside the Chinese economic and cultural context. Mastering Chinese will allow me to bridge both worlds — using English for global academic exchange and Chinese for deep market engagement.

Why China?

China is Vietnam’s largest trading partner, particularly in agriculture. Through my research on the “volume trap,” I became deeply aware of the structural challenges — declining unit values, weak brand equity, and limited value capture — that constrain Vietnam’s position in the Chinese market.

Studying in China will allow me to gain first-hand understanding of Chinese consumer markets, trade regulations, and value-chain dynamics. I hope to contribute to more strategic, higher-value cooperation between Vietnam and China in the years ahead.
